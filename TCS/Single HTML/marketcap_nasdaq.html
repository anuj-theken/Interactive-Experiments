Between 1992 and 1995, TCS expanded its software services footprint incrementally from Rs 2,175 million to Rs 5,206 million.

During this timeframe, international markets like the NASDAQ remained steady near 1,000 points, laying the groundwork for the upcoming tech boom.

As a result, the Y2K disaster was averted. With no exaggeration, Indian firms, led by TCS, played a major role in facilitating humankind’s technological transition from the second to third millennium without major disruptions.

By 1999, TCS revenue reached Rs 21,146 million, while NASDAQ surged to 4,069 during the peak of the dot-com rally.

TCS then missed the dot-com boom. Some of its rivals built flashy websites and e-commerce applications while TCS was buried in mainframe code.

This made it perfectly positioned for the bust that followed in 2001, when internet companies' valuations collapsed and turned to India to cut costs.
