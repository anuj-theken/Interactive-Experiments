Between 1992 and 1995, TCS expanded its software services footprint incrementally from Rs 2,175 million to Rs 5,206 million.

During this timeframe, the NASDAQ remained steady near 1,000 points, laying the groundwork for the tech boom ahead.

As a result, the Y2K disaster was averted. Indian firms, led by TCS, played a major role in facilitating a technology transition across the world's systems without major disruption.

By 1999, TCS revenue reached Rs 21,146 million, while the NASDAQ surged to 4,069 during the peak of the dot-com rally.

TCS then missed the dot-com boom outright — rivals built flashy websites and e-commerce applications while TCS stayed buried in mainframe code.

That positioned it well for the bust that followed in 2001, when internet valuations collapsed and companies turned to India to cut costs.

“Trust built during Y2K converted into long-term maintenance contracts.”
