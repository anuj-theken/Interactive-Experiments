Chapter 02

Two migrations, one city

The Great Migration

How the Parsis and Tamil Brahmins migrated across India.

The Parsi Migration

Descendants of Zoroastrian refugees who fled Persia after the Arab conquest, they sailed through the Persian Gulf and Arabian Sea to settle on India's west coast (Gujarat) as farmers and traders.

Tamil Brahmin Routes

Simultaneously, Tamil Brahmins moved from their roots in the Kaveri delta outward through major corridors toward booming hubs like Bombay, Bangalore, and Chennai.

Bombay Industrialization

The Parsis moved down the Konkan coast into Bombay, building the docks, pioneering cotton mills, and founding modern industrial giants like the Tata Group.

Source: Historical migration scholarship; Tata Group company history.

First TCS Contract

To meet its exports obligation, TCS provided software services to Burroughs — migrating old mainframe software.
First job: converting a hospital accounting package from Medium Systems COBOL to Small Systems COBOL.
The contract was worth $24,000.
Engineers in Detroit sent manuals and code to Bombay.
Engineers in Bombay wrote a programme to translate the code.
It ran on an ICL mainframe — bought cheap from LIC after unions blocked its use.
This seeded TCS's model: develop in India, implement globally.
"That's how the Indian software industry was born — not by design, but by accident."

Part One · New York

The 24/7 Advantage

With clients in New York, S Ramadorai and TCS realised that the time zone difference was an advantage. It was possible to work 24 hours a day, 7 days a week.

Part Two · Bombay

Follow the Sun

This led to the offshore-onshore method: TCS engineers in India work while the US sleeps. The team could “follow the sun” and work round the clock, like a mainframe that never shuts down.

Part Three · The World

A Global Footprint

TCS’s team on the IGIC contract grew to 300–400 over ten years. Its way of working was to build close, long-term relationships with clients — learning their countries, cultures, markets and products. Its work for American Express alone took TCS to Arizona, Florida, Argentina, the United Kingdom, Australia and Yugoslavia.

Chapter — The Y2K Bug

The Y2K phenomenon

When the world was crashing, TCS saved the day

Source: TCS Archives; NASSCOM Y2K remediation records.

A steady rise in TCS's revenue

Revenue (Rs million) NASDAQ Composite
1992 – 1995 Scroll to continue ↓

Source: TCS Annual Reports; NASDAQ Composite historical index. Pre-FY2011 TCS revenue is drawn from period filings — a NASDAQ-comparable revenue series is only consistently available for FY2011–FY2026 in company filings.

Between 1992 and 1995, TCS expanded its software services footprint incrementally from Rs 2,175 million to Rs 5,206 million.

During this timeframe, the NASDAQ remained steady near 1,000 points, laying the groundwork for the tech boom ahead.

As a result, the Y2K disaster was averted. Indian firms, led by TCS, played a major role in facilitating a technology transition across the world's systems without major disruption.

By 1999, TCS revenue reached Rs 21,146 million, while the NASDAQ surged to 4,069 during the peak of the dot-com rally.

TCS then missed the dot-com boom outright — rivals built flashy websites and e-commerce applications while TCS stayed buried in mainframe code.

That positioned it well for the bust that followed in 2001, when internet valuations collapsed and companies turned to India to cut costs.

“Trust built during Y2K converted into long-term maintenance contracts.”
