Chidambaram 2013, Modi 2026: the same speech, thirteen years apart

1 Mar 2013: "I'm hoping that the people of India will heed my appeal and will not demand so much gold." 10 May 2026: reconsider "excessive gold purchases for a year." Both followed by duty hikes; both during rupee crises (CAD 5.4% then; USD/INR 96.84 now). BJP's Harsh Sanghavi tweeted a pre-emptive contrast on the day.

Why it works. A "who said it" quiz (Morarji Desai 1963, Chidambaram 2013, Rajan 2014, Modi 2015 on "idle gold", Modi 2026) would be the cheapest high-share interaction in the piece. D. Subbarao, RBI governor in 2013, writes op-eds and is on X.

A4 · The 2026 moment · Rohin’s call alone.

The RBI hoards while telling citizens to stop

RBI holds 880.5 t (Mar 2026), 77% now in Mumbai and Nagpur after flying ~380 t home from the Bank of England since 2022 (168 t in FY26 alone). Gold is 15.7% of reserves, up from 5.9% in 2021, but almost entirely via price: RBI bought 73 t in 2024, 4 t in 2025 and 0.2 t in Q2 2026.

Why it works. The irony is obvious; the deeper story is why RBI stopped buying just before the appeal, and whether the 2024 repatriation (announced on Dhanteras) was a hedge against sanctions risk.

The family-gold calculator: what your wedding gold is worth, against every alternative

₹63.25/10g in 1964, ₹1,330 in 1980, ₹3,200 in 1990, ₹4,400 in 2000, ₹12,500 in 2008, ₹1.53 lakh today: a 62-year CAGR of ~13.4%. A retail year table exists from 1964; the RBI Handbook series is annual from FY1978-79 and monthly from 1990; SGB issue prices give dated official anchors for 2015–24. Nobody in India, or anywhere, has built the interactive version of "gold in your birth year".

Why it works. The engine of the whole piece. Inputs: a wedding year (or birth year) and grams, or a "few bangles" tier. Outputs: today's value vs FD, Sensex, PPF, USD cash, CPI. Then the twist (B4). Ships as a WhatsApp card.

Gold beats Nifty on every window to 20 years; loses at 25 to 40

3-yr 38.4% vs 10.0%; 5-yr 28.4% vs 10.0%; 10-yr 18.1% vs 12.5%; 20-yr 15.1% vs 11.2% (Nifty 50 TRI). 40-yr from Dec 1985: Sensex 13.6%, gold 12.1%, silver 11.4%, G-secs 9.1%. B4 · Gold as money · Also on Anuj’s list.

Forty percent of your gold's return is just the rupee falling

1964–2026: gold in dollars ~8.1%/yr, USD/INR ~4.9%/yr, gold in rupees ~13.4%/yr. Gold has never had a negative decade in rupees; in dollars it had two (1980s, 1990s). In 2026 gold is roughly flat in dollars and +17% in rupees. Rupee: 4.76 (1964), 7.50 (1966), 44 (2000), 68.8 (2013), 96.84 (May 2026).

Why it works. The reveal after the calculator: a slider that strips the rupee out of the return. It reframes "Indians love gold" as "Indians distrust the rupee", which is the thesis in T2. Value Research's line that gold's job is to "hold its value when the currency weakens" is the plain-English version.

The government shorted gold and lost ₹1.5 lakh crore

Sovereign Gold Bonds: 67 tranches, 147 t, ₹72,275 cr raised (Nov 2015–Feb 2024), then quietly discontinued. Outstanding ~126–132 t; estimated loss ₹1.3 lakh cr (IIFL) to ₹1.5 lakh cr (Subhash Garg, ex-Finance Secretary). 2017-18 Series XIV redeemed at +387% in Jan 2026. Economic Affairs Secretary Ajay Seth called it "fairly high-cost borrowing". Two more 8-year maturities fall on ~23 Oct and ~13 Nov 2026, either side of our launch.

Why it works. A scheme meant to curb imports became "a call option on gold sold without a hedge". An SGB windfall counter, or interviewing a 2016 buyer cashing out at 4×, is a vivid way in. Garg is reachable.

Households hold 30× the RBI and 4× Fort Knox

C3 · The hoard · Also on Anuj’s list.

Gold is 11% of Indian household wealth; in China it's 0.4%

RBI Household Finance Committee (Ramadorai, 2017): real estate 77%, gold 11%, durables 7%, financial assets 5%, retirement ~0%. Poorest quintile: ~20% in gold. Dvara's rural survey: 99% of low-income households own gold; one in ten buys every year; daughters' weddings the top reason. Caveat found: AIDIS 2019 has no gold block at all, so how the 11% was imputed needs checking.

Why it works. The comparison with China, the world's other great gold buyer, is the sharpest single line in the cluster. Ramadorai answers email.

Only ~7–8% of India's gold is pledged. What if it were 20%?

Organised gold-loan AUM ~₹18–19 lakh crore at ~65% LTV implies ~2,500 t pledged, ~7% of the stock (MOFSL says ~8% "monetised"). Manappuram alone holds 67 t in its vaults, more than most central banks. The unorganised pawnbroker market is another 65% of loans by count at 30–50% interest.

Why it works. The counterfactual question the economists never answer with data. Good for a short, provocative section; needs Muthoot/Manappuram tonnage and one economist willing to guess.

The Kerala bride carries 320 g; the average Indian wedding uses 28 g

Bridal gold is 50–55% of jewellery demand by weight. WGC's own wedding count disagrees with itself (8–10 m vs 11–13 m a year; CAIT counts 46 lakh in a single six-week season). D4 · Culture, region, gender · Also on Anuj’s list.

500 g, 250 g, 100 g: the tax code's quiet theory of who owns gold

CBDT Instruction 1916 (11 May 1994) tells search officers not to seize up to 500 g per married woman, 250 g per unmarried woman and 100 g per man. It's a seizure guideline, not a holding limit, but high courts have treated it as a presumption of legitimate source. The state codifies that a woman's normal endowment is five times a man's.

Why it works. One paragraph, one great fact. Also the answer to the most-Googled gold question in India ("how much gold can I keep at home"), which is free search traffic.

Kerala's gold is made by Bengali hands

Thrissur: ~3,000 workshops, ~40,000 artisans, ~1 t/day of Kerala's consumption; 20,000+ migrant goldsmiths from Bengal and Odisha. D8 · Culture, region, gender · Also on Anuj’s list.

Pliny's complaint: the 2,000-year "sink of gold"

Pliny, <i>Natural History</i> 12.84 (c. 77 CE): India, China and Arabia drain Rome of 100 million sesterces a year, "the price our luxuries and our women cost us"; 6.101 gives 50 million for India alone. Roman aurei hoards cluster in Kerala and Tamil Nadu (the Kottayam hoard, ~8,000 coins). Then the 1930s reversal: 1,337 t of "distress gold" exported 1931–41 as peasants sold to survive the Depression, which the EPW's Vibha Iyer argues financed Britain's recovery.

Why it works. Colour for the opening or a history interlude. The 1930s outflow is the one time India was a net seller, and a strong counterpoint to 2026.

Half the gold at Kalyan's counter is the customer's own

Kalyan: exchanged old gold was 46% of revenue in Q1 FY27 and 55% in June 2026. Senco: 43% of sales by weight via exchange. Titan: exchange is ~50% of sourcing, up from 40%. WGC: old-gold exchange is 40–70% of transactions in some stores. Yet formal recycling collapsed to 19 t in Q2 2026 because Indians would rather pledge than sell. Why it works. The jeweller is becoming a recycler earning making charges on metal it never bought. Ties E1 to the tax angle (D4, G) and to "gold changing shape" (T5).

E2 · The business · Also on Anuj’s list.

The jewellery bill decoder: what you lose the moment you buy

Making charges run 3–30% (Tanishq 8–25%, Malabar 16–26%, Kalyan 5–30%), plus 3% GST on the piece and 5% on itemised making. A 5 g 22K ring at ₹58,173 all-in carries ₹8,930 (15.3%) you never get back. Coins lose ~3%; ETFs 0.2–1% a year. The "pay 11 months, 12th free" schemes are capped at 11 months because the Companies Act's 365-day rule turns them into deposits on day 366; the "free" 8.3% is recouped through making charges.

Why it works. Reader enters a bill (or picks a piece), sees the split. Consumer-useful, screenshot-friendly, and it makes the point about T5 without lecturing.

History

F1 · History · Rohin’s call alone.

1962–1990: the Gold Control Act banned 14K and built Bombay's underworld

Morarji Desai's rules recalled bank gold loans (1962), banned jewellery above 14 carat (1963), and by 1968 barred citizens from owning bars and coins. The 1962 gold bond raised 2.7 t against a 1,746 t target: 0.15%. Goldsmiths lost livelihoods overnight; smuggling via Dubai dhows built Haji Mastan and then Dawood; Bollywood made the smuggler a hero (Deewaar, 1975). Repealed 6 June 1990.

Why it works. The first act of T3. The irony that 14K was once illegal and is now "par for the course" (E7) is a gift. Hussain Zaidi (Dongri to Dubai) and the IIMA "Glittering Paradox" paper.

F5 · History · Rohin’s call alone.

The 1930s: the only decade India sold its gold, and it bailed out Britain

After sterling left gold in 1931, India exported 1,337 t over ten years as peasants sold to survive the Depression. Viceroy Willingdon: "For the first time in history... Indians are disgorging gold." Vibha Iyer (EPW 2023) argues these were distress asset sales that should count as capital outflows, financing Britain's recovery.

Why it works. The counter-example that proves the rule: Indians sell gold only under duress, and when they do, someone else benefits. Balachandran's <i>John Bullion's Empire</i> is the monograph.

Central banks bought 1,000+ tonnes a year for three years, then India stopped and Poland didn't

Central bank buying: 255 t (2020) → 1,080 t (2022) → 1,051 → 1,089 → 863 t (2025). Poland now holds 640 t (28% of reserves, target 700). China 2,366 t. WGC's 2026 survey: 74% of central banks expect a smaller dollar share in five years. Gold passed the euro as the second reserve asset in 2025. JPMorgan's target: $6,000 by end-2026.

Why it works. Context for A4: the RBI bought with the herd in 2024 and then stopped as the herd continued. Also the "de-dollarisation" frame Indian readers will expect.
