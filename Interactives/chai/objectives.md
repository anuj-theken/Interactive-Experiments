# How do you make your tea?

## 1. Who's the reader, and why would they share it?

The core reader is the First Principles community: curious professionals, mostly in Indian cities, who already read FP on Sundays. Only ~200 of them participated in the poll. Each week, ~100,000 people open FP emails. So, our goal is to target and engage them. Can we get at least 25,000 of them to click and engage with our story?

The wider reader is anyone who drinks tea, which in India is almost everyone. But our best route to them is via shares done by our primary audience.

People will share it for a few reasons:
(a) because everyone believes their own tea is the correct one. I want each reader to see their cup sitting among everyone else's, and then send it to the person at home who makes it "wrong".
(b) because everyone likes to see their own preferences in the context of broader preferences, preferably with some justification ("Your recipe is unique").

## 2. Why did I want to do this survey?

I know my tea recipe and method is arduous and I've never met anyone else who makes it exactly like that. It's almost like a cultural fingerprint for Indians, who love their tea.

This got me curious about how many other variations we could uncover, and in the process learn about possible "improvements".

<u>This is important:</u> I want this to become the "seed" for a "how India makes chai" project that amasses thousands of responses in a year, and over the years.

## 3. What do I expect the results to show?

(a) Clusters of recipes/methods, and then the ability to explore those that are "near" me. 

(b) Explanations for why people add certain things or steps. How many of them are sheer family habits versus conscious additions. 

(c) A unique "formula" for every recipe, including mine, that I can share. Ideally, a custom-generated image of different types of chai, and mine. So I can share and feel a bit "intellectual".

## 4. Which survey questions am I most excited about?

- **How many cups do you usually make at a time?** Because it gives a sense of how social tea is.
- **The per-spice detail** (roasted, crushed, seeds only). This is where the obsessives reveal themselves, and I want to celebrate them.
- **The "when does it go in?" questions** for milk, tea, sweetener and spices. They are the sequence, and the sequence is the story.
- **"How many times does it boil after the milk?"** Every Indian household has a view on this.
- **"Describe it in your own words."** That's where the best quotes will come from.

## 5. Tone

Warm, curious, a little nerdy about the formula. It should feel like an argument over chai in the kitchen. The reader should never feel judged for how they make their tea.

Reference story: (tone, not creative interpretation) https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2014/upshot/dialect-quiz-map.html

## 6. How should this feel different from Best Decade?

Best Decade asked about life and age, and it was reflective, even a bit wistful. This one is about a daily habit, so it can be lighter and more playful.

- **Chart style:** I'd like the cup/mug to replace the square as the unit. Each reader becomes a cup, and the cup's visual properties come straight from their recipe (see the encoding below). The sequence of steps needs its own chart. 
- **Structure:** Build the reader's tea step by step, the way they make it: liquid, then tea, then extras, then timing. Their cup appears at the end, sorted into a family of similar teas. I'm happy to be the "meet one reader" guide this time, using my own cup.
- **Tone:** The kitchen, not the therapist's couch.

**Cup encoding (from the survey design):**

| Recipe element | How it shows on the cup |
|---|---|
| Milk share, tea amount, brew time | Liquid colour, computed from the answers, never chosen |
| Number of boils after milk | Froth band or steam wisps |
| Extras (ginger, cardamom…) | Small icons at the rim. Shape shows form: whole, crushed, seeds, roasted |
| Sweetener | Sugar cubes on the saucer. A struck-through cube means "no sugar" |
| Sequence | The saucer as a clock face: ingredients placed clockwise in the order they go in, with flame marks for boils and a mark for heat-off |
| Vessel | Glass, cup and saucer, mug, steel tumbler |

Every answer reduces to a formula with five stages (start, heating, boil, heat off, in the cup). Where two ingredients share a stage, treat them as going in together.

## 7. How should the reader feel at the end?

Recognised. Their tea is theirs, and it turns out to have relatives they didn't know about. 

Smarter. They know why and how tea is made in such a infinite number of ways.

Brimming with ideas about tea hacks. Ideally they're tempted to try someone else's recipe the next morning.

## 8. What to avoid

- Pie charts and bar graphs that state the obvious. I said this about the coffee survey and I still mean it.
- Any sense of ranking: no "best tea", no "you're doing it wrong" (except as a joke the reader makes about someone else).
- Reusing the Best Decade squares and scrollytelling beat for beat. Some continuity is fine, but this should look like its own piece.

Q: Any format you're personally tired of seeing. Yes, zero-interaction, lazy scrollytelling that assumes/pre-supposes users won't lift a finger.
