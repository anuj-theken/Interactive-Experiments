How do you make your chai?

Every Indian kitchen is quietly certain its tea is the correct one. 194 readers told us exactly how they make theirs: what goes in, when, and how many times it boils. Build your cup and meet its relatives.

byRohin Dharmakumar and Anuj Debnath

I know my tea recipe is arduous, and I’ve never met anyone who makes it exactly the way I do. Everyone at home has an opinion about it. Everyone at home is wrong, obviously.

So we asked. Readers of First Principles told us their milk, their leaf, their spices and, most importantly, their sequence: when each thing goes in, and how many times it’s allowed to boil. Of 194 recipes, 190 were one of a kind.

Now make yours. Tap an ingredient to put it in your pan, tap its icon to take it out, and keep scrolling: the clock moves, the pan heats, and every chart you meet will show where your cup sits. Your recipe stays in your browser; nothing is saved or sent.

About the data

194 readers of First Principles answered the survey between 25 October and 3 November 2026. It is not a representative sample of India; most respondents live in Bengaluru, Mumbai or Delhi NCR, and about two in three are men. The survey is still open, and this page will grow as more cups come in.

Shades are computed from each reader’s answers (milk share, amount of tea, brewing time and number of boils), never chosen. Where a reader didn’t say when a spice goes in, we used the time they gave for all their spices. Families are simple rules, not a clustering model. Readers are identified only by age band and town; some quotes are lightly trimmed.
