Are your best years behind you?

We asked 301 readers which decade of their life has been the happiest, when they first felt middle-aged, and at what age they would stop ageing if they could. Their answers follow a pattern.

Chapter one

The happiest decade

In September, we asked readers of First Principles a few questions about getting older. Which decade of your life has been your happiest so far? Which one do you expect to be your happiest? Have you ever felt middle-aged?

301 of you answered. Here is what you told us, one reader at a time.

301Every dot here is one reader who took our survey.
Most of you are in the middle of your lives. 77% are in your 30s, 40s or 50s. Readers in their 30s are the largest group.
We asked which decade has been your happiest so far. For almost half of you (49%), the answer was the decade you're in now. Most of the rest named an earlier one.
Move each reader to the decade they named, and two decades stand out: the 20s and the 30s. Together they get 58% of the answers.Red dots are still readers who named the decade they're in now.
Then we asked which decade you expect to be your happiest. The answers move later. The 50s get more votes than any other decade. Only 12 readers picked their 20s.
Put the two answers side by side. 6 in 10 of you think your best decade is still ahead. About a third say it's now. Only 17 readers think it's behind them.Even among readers in their 50s, almost nobody thinks it's behind them.

Then, at some point, something changes.

Chapter two

The day you felt middle-aged

Middle age doesn't start on a fixed date. It's more a feeling, and it tends to arrive suddenly. So we asked: have you ever felt middle-aged? If so, at what age, and what happened?

Each dot is one reader. Hover over a dot to see that reader's answers.
132 of you said yes, you have felt middle-aged. That's about one in six readers in their 20s, and more than two in three in their 50s.
We asked when it first happened. Half of you had felt it by 43.124 readers gave an age.
Notice the tall stacks. 35, 40, 45, 50, 55. More than half of all the ages given end in a 0 or a 5. Milestone birthdays seem to do a lot of the work.
What actually happened? For almost half of the readers who told us, it was their own body: an injury that wouldn't heal, a diabetes diagnosis, a metabolism that seemed to switch off at 40.
Next came children growing up, and parents growing old. And over and over, one moment: a child calling you "uncle".

Chapter three

The pill

Last question. Imagine a pill that stops biological ageing in its tracks. You stay the age you were when you took it, for good. At what age would you take it?

Each dot is one reader. Hover over a dot to see that reader's answers.
272 of you gave an age. The answers run from 10 to 110.
35This is both the most common answer and the median: 39 readers would stop at exactly 35. 59% of readers would stop before 40.
Round numbers again. 60% of the answers end in a 0 or a 5. We seem to think about our lives in five-year blocks.
Your answer depends on how old you are now. Readers in their 20s would stop at around 28, inside the decade they're in. Readers in their 50s would go back to 45, and readers in their 70s to 60. Red dots are readers who would go back to an age they have already passed.Grey bands show the decade each group is in now.
And then there are the four readers who said 99, 100 or 110. One of them wrote: “Wouldn't take the pill actually.”

Chapter four

What makes a decade good

We also gave readers a list of things that could make a decade good and asked them to pick as many as they wanted. Freedom came first, just ahead of relationships. Work was in the middle of the list. Learning came last.

What a good decade needs
% of the 271 readers who picked at least one option

Put together, the answers show a pattern. Most readers think their happiest decade so far is a recent one, and most expect the happiest one to come later still. Most would freeze themselves around 35 if they could. And middle age doesn't come with a particular birthday. It comes when your knees, your children or your younger colleagues start to remind you of your age.

About the data

301 readers of First Principles answered the survey between 13 and 22 September 2026. It is not a representative sample. 72% of respondents are men, and most live in Bengaluru, Mumbai or Delhi NCR.

Percentages leave out readers who skipped a question or said it was too close to call. If a reader said they expected their happiest decade to be “the one I'm in now”, we counted it as the decade they are in. Themes in the middle-age answers were assigned by keyword matching, so a single answer can count towards more than one theme. We quote only readers who agreed to be quoted, and we identify them only by decade and city.
