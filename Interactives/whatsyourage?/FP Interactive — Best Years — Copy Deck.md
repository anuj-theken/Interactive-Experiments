`[STEP 3]` Now sort them. Each column is how old readers are now; the higher a square, the older they'd be when they took the pill. Half would stop the clock by **35**, the red line.
*The dotted diagonal marks their age now. Our reader would stop at 30.*

`[STEP 4]` **117 readers**, 43%, would **go back**: they'd take the pill at an age younger than they are now. The older the reader, the more likely: 12% of readers in their 20s, but 60% of those in their 50s.
*Our reader is one of them. We only know each reader's decade, so "younger than now" means before the decade they're in.*

`[STEP 5]` Only **43** would **wait**, picking an age older than they are now. Most of them are young: 41% of readers in their 20s would wait, but just 5% of those in their 50s.
*The other 106 would stop somewhere in the decade they're in now.*

`[STEP 6]` And then there are the **four readers** who said 99, 100 or 110. One of them wrote: "Wouldn't take the pill actually."

The "perfect age", it would seem from responses, is ahead of you when you're young. Then, as you approach middle-age, it's close to where you are. Then, as you get older, its behind you.

ROHIN: When I wrote about this in September, I leaned on the U-bend: the finding, repeated country after country, that happiness is high in our 20s, sinks through our 30s and 40s, bottoms out somewhere around the mid-life crisis, and then climbs again, even as we lose the things we spend our youth protecting. Laura Carstensen tells her Stanford undergrads every year that these are not the best years of their lives, and watches the relief on their faces. The Economist put it more bluntly: life is not a long slow decline from sunlit uplands towards the valley of death.

`[ABOUT THE DATA]`
301 readers of First Principles answered the survey between 13 and 22 September 2026. It is not a representative sample. 72% of respondents are men, and most live in Bengaluru, Mumbai or Delhi NCR.

Percentages leave out readers who skipped a question or said it was too close to call. If a reader said they expected their happiest decade to be "the one I'm in now", we counted it as the decade they are in. Themes in the middle-age answers were assigned by keyword matching, so a single answer can count towards more than one theme.
