Moodboard palette

The five swatches off the top of the board, verbatim

These five are the board's own strip, unmodified. They are two reds, a cream ground and two blues — surface, type and ornament colours, not a chart palette. Oxblood and Deep Navy are too dark to read as series, Steel is too grey, and the two reds are one hue (§04 covers what the charts use instead).

05

Chart aesthetic

Printed, not dashboard

The board's charts look like they came off a press: flat ink, square ends, hatched remainders, big numbers on leader lines. No gradients, no shadows, no rounded bar caps. These four forms come straight from the references and should be the first reach.

Half-donut with leader callouts
Share-of-whole, ≤ 4 parts. Flat edge on a 1px navy baseline, 2px paper gaps between wedges. Vermilion picks out the one part the story is about.
Concentric rings, hatched tracks
A few independent shares, one ring each. The unfilled track is the navy hatch, not grey. Dotted 1px leaders run out to right-aligned figures.

Two-pole block chart
Blue vs red is the board's core contrast. Sub-groups take tints from the diverging arms — darkest step for the largest group — so the two poles never mix.

Bars and segments end square (borderRadius: 0) — this overrides the generic rounded-cap default. Gridlines are optional: prefer none plus direct labels, or the graph-paper ground. When an axis is needed, draw the baseline only, 1px Deep Navy.

A 22px warm sand grid on Paper, standing in for chart gridlines. Recessive — the grid never competes with the mark drawn over it.
Chapter 03 The road out of Madras
Navy chapter panel
Full-bleed Deep Navy to break the paper run and mark a new act. Steel eyebrow, Cream heading. Charts inside switch to the dark palette.
Pull quote One line, set big, in cream on red
Signboard panel
From the hand-painted Tamil signs: Crimson ground, Cream type, an inset cream keyline. One per story, for a pull-quote or hero stat — never a chart.

Cards are flat in this system — a hairline --color-rule border on a warm surface over the Paper ground. The board's references are printed pieces; drop shadows do not belong here. Keep the frame wrapper anyway (see the prompt guide) — it owns the card's padding, and any module that does opt into a shadow still needs it.
