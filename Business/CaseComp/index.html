Survey findings · The WhatsApp Life

Where does everyday life actually hurt?

We asked people to scroll back through their own WhatsApp chats and point to the tasks that felt frustrating, procrastinated, or repetitive. 469 respondents tagged real moments across 14 areas of life and wrote 1,291 short stories about them. Ten charts below walk through what the data says — what hurts most, why, who it hurts, and what people are already trying (or not trying at all) to fix it.

01 / Demand

Money is the headline pain point — by a wide margin

We asked"Scroll back through your WhatsApp — which of these 14 areas of life turned up a task that felt frustrating, procrastinated, or repetitive? (pick all that apply)"

Ranked by how many people picked each life area as a pain point, money, investing and insurance leads at 225 selections — nearly a third more than second-place Groceries (165). Fitness (138) and Shopping (123) round out the top four. This is where demand concentrates.

02 / Vocabulary

Every pain point is one of three feelings

We askedFor every area you picked: "Which of these fits best — Frustrating, Procrastinate, or Repetitive?"

Respondents tagged each pain point as Frustrating (a fight every time), Procrastinate (avoided, barely done) or Repetitive (mindless, recurring). Frustration dominates — 602 of 1,295 tags, well ahead of Procrastinate (385) and Repetitive (308). This vocabulary carries through every chart below.

03 / The cross

Different categories need different kinds of fixes

CombiningWhich areas people picked (chart 01) with which feeling they gave each one (chart 02) — every category crossed against Frustrating / Procrastinate / Repetitive.

Crossing emotion against category reveals what builders actually need to design for. Groceries is repetitive, not frustrating (78 vs 77) — it needs automation, not empathy. Fitness and Learning are procrastination traps (76 and 56) — they need nudges, not tools. Money splits across all three. Getting-around and Travel are almost purely frustrating — they need the friction removed, not softened.

04 / Opportunity

Popular isn't the same as painful

CombiningHow often a category was picked (chart 01) against the share of its tags that came back Frustrating (chart 02/03) — every category plotted at once.

Plotting how often a category was picked against how frustrating it is finds the categories big enough to matter — and the outliers. Color marks which quadrant each category falls into against the sample average: orange is popular and painful, aqua is niche but painful, blue is popular but more manageable, and grey is both niche and mild. Weddings is picked rarely (38 times) but rated the most frustrating category on the page (71%) — a small, sharp, underserved pocket of pain sitting alone in the niche-but-painful corner.

05 / In their own words

"Time" is the word people reach for

We asked"Can you share an exact task behind that feeling? Where were you, what happened, what did you have to deal with?"

Across the 1,291 free-text stories, "time" appears 263 times — nearly twice as often as any other word — followed by need, find, different, order, want: the vocabulary of choice-overload and repetition, not of any single task going wrong. Split each word by the category it came from and the picture sharpens further — "order" is a Groceries word (56 of 105 mentions), while "time" and "every" lean hardest on Money. The rest ("Other") is spread thinly across the remaining ten categories.

06 / Status quo

Most people are duct-taping this together — or doing nothing

We asked"What's the system you've adopted to handle this today? Is it people (you, family, house staff, a broker or agent), apps, the chats, or nothing at all?"

Asked what system they use today, apps lead the mentions — but "nothing at all" is the second most common answer, ahead of family, chats, friends or a spreadsheet. That gap between "apps" and "nothing" is the real opportunity signal: a large share of this pain currently has no system at all.

07 / Who answered — age

A young-adult sample, median age 33

We asked"How old are you?" (a few junk entries like "937" and "12" were excluded)

The sample is concentrated in working-age adults — median age 33. The 25–34s log the most tags of any group by far, and frustration climbs steadily with age — the youngest respondents split their feelings fairly evenly, while 45–54s report the highest share of Frustrating tags of any band. Looking at the top categories by age, money and groceries pain shows up early and stays high through the 25–44 range, while younger respondents lean relatively more toward learning and shopping.

08 / Who answered — geography

Strong reach across India's biggest metros

We asked"Finally, which city do you live in?"

The survey reached deep into India's largest, most digitally-active cities — Bangalore (114) and Mumbai (99) together account for nearly half the sample, with Delhi, Hyderabad and Chennai making up most of the rest. That's exactly the audience this research set out to reach: a tier-1, always-online population living the pain points above in real time. Read every finding on this page as a tier-1 metro picture.

09 / Intensity signal

Emotional pain makes people write more

We asked"Can you share an exact task behind that feeling. Where were you, what happened, what did you have to deal with?" — measured here by how many words people wrote back (one-word non-answers like "N/A" excluded).

Stories tagged Frustrating or Procrastinate run a little longer on average than stories tagged Repetitive — a small gap, but a consistent one: when something genuinely bothers people, they explain it; repetitive drudgery gets a shorter shrug.
